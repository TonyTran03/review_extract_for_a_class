    Also can't leave Montreal without trying poutine! Heard this place is better than La Banquise so stopped by for lunch. When you enter, you first place your order with one of the servers. Then you make your way to the cashier area and pay when your order is ready. There are several tables but this place is popular so plan for takeout if you come during the lunch rush. The menu is entirely in French but if you're here for the poutine, which most people are, it's #8 on the menu and you have the option of ordering either the small or large size. Unless you come with a party of more than 5, would recommend getting the small because even that is a huge portion. We did see a group of teenagers order a small each, which had us worried because it's basically a heart attack on a plate. Very heavy, greasy, and salty. Something I would try once but probably not have again in the near future. Sort of like the case with Chicago deep dish pizza haha. I did enjoy the rotisserie chicken and cheese on top of the fries though but was not a fan of the chorizo because it was too hard and salty.

    Located in the Plateau Mont-Royal neighborhood of town, MA Poulle Mouillée is a very popular, no-frills spot that's as famous for it's insanely long lines and wait times as it is for their succulent Portuguese style chicken.

    And while trying to get that aforementioned chicken can easily require standing in a line anywhere from 45 minutes to 90 minutes, most loyalists would say that it's absolutely worth the trouble and I would agree.

    Jean-Jacques Rousseau once said "Patience is bitter, but its fruit is sweet" and boy was he right. The chicken coming out of this place is absolutely insane. It's full of flavor, it's moist and it is one of the bites of food I've had in a very long time. Depending on what you order, the contents of your box might change a bit but I recommend you get the "Le Tremblay" option for $16CAD which features some chicken breast, a ton of fries and a side salad of greens with shaved carrots in a delicious vinaigrette. Don't share, just chow down in shame. It's greasy, it's tasty and full of flavor.

    If you're super hungry (you must be American - hi!) you can get other, larger options: ones with chorizo or even befana and while the first is scrumptious, the befana was nothing to write home about and ordering it isn't something I'd do again. Honestly the Tremblay is plenty of food.

    Service wasn't particularly friendly or fast - but man, it just doesn't matter. Just get in line and you'll be rewarded with a Portuguese feast which shines some spotlight on a cultural piece of Montreal that few other places can showcase as well. A+
